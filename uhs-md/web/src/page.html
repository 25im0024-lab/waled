What you are looking at

A 3D slit pore in calcite (walls at the bottom and top, periodic sideways; the blue frame is one periodic cell). On a water-wet carbonate the walls carry a film of formation water (blue beads, grey beads = dissolved NaCl). Free gas fills the centre: H₂ (white) mixed with the cushion gas. Dissolved gas molecules are drawn enlarged. Green rods are methanogenic microbes on the walls; a microbe turns bright once it holds 4 H₂. Untick "Water" or use "Slice" to see inside. In the live simulator the calcite lattice is drawn schematically; in "LAMMPS run" every atom is real.

Experiments to try

  • Cushion-gas mixing. Run "Withdraw" and watch the produced-gas purity. A CO₂ cushion stays mixed with H₂ in the pore. Raising the cushion fraction lowers the purity of recovered H₂.
  • Dissolution loss. Compare the dissolved H₂ count with a CO₂ cushion. CO₂ dissolves far more readily than H₂, CH₄ or N₂, so it is lost to the brine first.
  • Salting-out. Raise salinity from 0 to 4 mol/kg and rebuild: fewer gas molecules dissolve.
  • Microbial loss. Switch on the microbes. Every 4 H₂ + 1 CO₂ become one CH₄. Five gas molecules turn into one, so the pressure drops and H₂ purity falls. With a CH₄ or N₂ cushion, untick "CO₂ supply from carbonate" and methanogenesis stalls once the H₂ store is full: carbon is the limiting reactant.
  • Wettability. Lower the calcite wettability (less water-wet): the brine film thins and gas reaches the calcite surface.

Physics

  • Dynamics: 3D, velocity-Verlet, 2 fs time step, Berendsen thermostat (0.2 ps), Verlet neighbour list, 8.5 Å cutoff. Periodic in x and y (40 Å), calcite walls at z = 0 and z = H.
  • Gas molecules (one LJ site each): H₂ σ = 2.96 Å, ε/k = 34.2 K (Buch 1994); CH₄ 3.73 Å, 148 K (TraPPE-UA); CO₂ 3.941 Å, 195.2 K and N₂ 3.798 Å, 71.4 K (one-site viscosity values, Svehla 1962).
  • Brine: coarse-grained liquid beads (σ = 3.1 Å, ε/k = 420 K). Each bead stands for one water molecule; one salt bead stands for one dissolved Na⁺Cl⁻ pair. The salt binds water more strongly and excludes gas, which produces salting-out.
  • Gas–water coupling: Lorentz-Berthelot with kij = 0.45 (H₂), 0.47 (N₂), 0.42 (CH₄) and 0.12 (CO₂). The smaller CO₂ value stands in for the quadrupole a one-site model lacks, and gives the observed solubility order CO₂ ≫ CH₄ > N₂ ≈ H₂.
  • Calcite walls: 9-3 Lennard-Jones wall potentials (an integrated LJ half-space). "Wettability" sets the water–wall well depth.
  • Gas loading: Peng-Robinson density at (T, P) for the feed composition.
  • Bacteria: fixed microbes. Each takes up an H₂ molecule within 7 Å with the set probability per step. After 4 H₂ it takes one CO₂ (from the gas or, if allowed, from carbonate) and releases CH₄ + 2 H₂O. Hydrogen, carbon and oxygen atoms are conserved exactly (checked by the test suite).

Limits: read before drawing conclusions

  • The live model is coarse-grained, so solubilities, diffusion coefficients and interfacial properties are qualitative. Trends and rankings are the output, not numbers. The "LAMMPS run" view is the atomistic model.
  • Microbial kinetics are accelerated by roughly 10¹⁰ or more: real methanogenesis takes hours to days, the simulation shows picoseconds.
  • There is no carbonate dissolution, pH or aqueous carbonate chemistry, and no electrostatics.
  • Sulfate reducers (H₂S), homoacetogens and biofilm/EPS are not included.
  • Small system (about a thousand molecules): expect fluctuations; average over time and repeat runs.
