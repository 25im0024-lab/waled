Teaching model. This is a 2D coarse-grained simulation: each molecule is one Lennard-Jones site, water is a liquid-forming bead, and bacterial activity is sped up by many orders of magnitude. Use it for trends and intuition. For quantitative work, use the full 3D LAMMPS model (tab "Full LAMMPS model" below).

What you are looking at

A slit pore in calcite (top and bottom walls). On a water-wet carbonate the walls carry a film of formation water (blue beads, grey beads = dissolved NaCl). Free gas fills the centre: H₂ (white) mixed with the cushion gas. Gas molecules drawn with a ring are dissolved in the brine. Green rods are methanogenic microbes attached to the walls; the dots on each rod show how much H₂ it has taken up.

Experiments to try

  • Cushion-gas mixing. Run "Withdraw" and watch the produced-gas purity. A CO₂ cushion stays mixed with H₂ in the pore. Raising the cushion fraction lowers the purity of recovered H₂.
  • Dissolution loss. Compare the dissolved H₂ count with a CO₂ cushion. CO₂ dissolves far more readily than H₂, CH₄ or N₂, so it is lost to the brine first.
  • Salting-out. Raise salinity from 0 to 4 mol/kg and rebuild: fewer gas molecules dissolve.
  • Microbial loss. Switch on the microbes. Every 4 H₂ + 1 CO₂ become one CH₄. Five gas molecules turn into one, so the pressure drops and H₂ purity falls. With a CH₄ or N₂ cushion, untick "CO₂ supply from carbonate" and methanogenesis stalls once the H₂ store is full: carbon is the limiting reactant.
  • Wettability. Lower the calcite wettability (less water-wet): the brine film thins and gas reaches the calcite surface.

Physics

  • Dynamics: velocity-Verlet, 2 fs time step, Berendsen thermostat (0.2 ps), cell lists, 10 Å cutoff. 2D, periodic along the pore.
  • Gas molecules (one LJ site each): H₂ σ = 2.96 Å, ε/k = 34.2 K (Buch 1994); CH₄ 3.73 Å, 148 K (TraPPE-UA); CO₂ 3.941 Å, 195.2 K and N₂ 3.798 Å, 71.4 K (one-site viscosity values, Svehla 1962).
  • Brine: coarse-grained liquid beads (σ = 3.1 Å, ε/k = 740 K). Each bead stands for one water molecule; one salt bead stands for one dissolved Na⁺Cl⁻ pair. The salt binds water more strongly and excludes gas, which produces salting-out.
  • Gas–water coupling: Lorentz-Berthelot with kij = 0.50 (H₂), 0.52 (N₂), 0.47 (CH₄) and 0.18 (CO₂). The smaller CO₂ value stands in for the quadrupole a one-site model lacks, and gives the observed solubility order CO₂ ≫ CH₄ > N₂ ≈ H₂.
  • Calcite walls: 10-4 Lennard-Jones wall potentials. "Wettability" sets the water–wall well depth.
  • Gas loading: Peng-Robinson density at (T, P) for the feed composition, mapped to 2D as ρ₂ = ρ₃2/3.
  • Bacteria: fixed microbes. Each takes up an H₂ molecule within 7 Å with the set probability per step. After 4 H₂ it takes one CO₂ (from the gas or, if allowed, from carbonate) and releases CH₄ + 2 H₂O. Hydrogen, carbon and oxygen atoms are conserved exactly (checked by the test suite).

Limits: read before drawing conclusions

  • The model is 2D and coarse-grained, so solubilities, diffusion coefficients and interfacial properties are qualitative. Trends and rankings are the output, not numbers.
  • Microbial kinetics are accelerated by roughly 10¹⁰ or more: real methanogenesis takes hours to days, the simulation shows picoseconds.
  • There is no carbonate dissolution, pH or aqueous carbonate chemistry, and no electrostatics.
  • Sulfate reducers (H₂S), homoacetogens and biofilm/EPS are not included.
  • Small system (a few hundred molecules): expect large fluctuations; average over time and repeat runs.
